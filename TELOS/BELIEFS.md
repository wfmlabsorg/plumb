# PLUMB Beliefs

## On Numbers

### Every Number Carries a Grade
`[M]` measured, read from data with its definition cited · `[C]` computed, formula stated ·
`[E]` estimated, range and assumption stated · `[A]` asserted, one source, never load-bearing
alone. A computed number inherits the weakest grade among its inputs. An ungraded number in a
report is unfalsifiable, and an unfalsifiable report cannot be challenged or trusted.

### A Point Estimate of Required Staff Is a Coin Flip Dressed as a Plan
Staffing to the mean means being understaffed half the time, and the cost of understaffing is not
symmetric with the cost of overstaffing. The range is not a caveat appended to the answer. It is
part of the answer.

### Shrinkage Belongs on the Supply Side, Once
Demand produces required *productive* hours. Supply converts heads into productive hours. Both
sides speak the same unit. Applying shrink twice — or not at all — is the most common defect in
this arithmetic, and because shrink is sampled, the error is largest in the draws that decide the
plan.

### We Calibrate the Forecast Error, Not the Forecast
Somebody else owns the volume forecast, and re-forecasting their business is neither our job nor
our competence. Knowing how wrong their forecast usually is, with a distribution attached, is.

## On Evidence

### Correlation Is Not Causation
Always specify the rung. Association (Rung 1) is necessary and never sufficient. Intervention
(Rung 2) and counterfactual (Rung 3) claims require an explicit DAG and a stated identification
strategy. In staffing series the confounder is usually sitting in plain sight in the event
calendar — look there before drawing anything.

### A Finding With No Disconfirming Condition Is an Assertion
Every finding names the test, the data, or the measurement that would change it. "What would move
this?" is not a courtesy at the end of an analysis; it is what makes the analysis a claim rather
than an opinion.

### Simpson's Paradox Is Real
Check within-group against between-group before claiming an effect. A whole-book contact rate that
holds steady while every segment's rate rises is a composition change, not a stable operation.

### Confidence Must Be Explicit
Never present an uncertain finding as certain. In a probabilistic model the discipline is
sharper than a confidence label: say which quantile, over what horizon, under which assumptions.

## On the System

### Scaffolding Over Model
The value is in the architecture — the schema, the mapping convention, the grade rule, the
standards — not in any single model call. Good scaffolding makes any capable model produce
defensible results.

### One Source of Truth for Any Piece of Arithmetic
Two implementations of the same formula diverge, and they diverge silently. This is why the
deterministic model and the Monte Carlo share an engine rather than mirroring each other.

### State the Weak Joint Rather Than Hope Nobody Asks
Occupancy as a service-level proxy, the weekly band beneath a daily plan, the parameters that
never learn. A model whose limitations are documented survives its first serious challenge. One
whose limitations are discovered by the audience does not.

### Analysis Is Pulled by a Question, Not Pushed by a Schedule
Running every analytical stage every day generates more findings than any planner will read, which
is the same as generating none.
