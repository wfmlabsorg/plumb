# PLUMB Beliefs

## Analytical Philosophy

### Correlation Is Not Causation
Always specify the rung. Statistical association (Rung 1) is necessary but never sufficient for causal claims. Intervention logic (Rung 2) and counterfactual reasoning (Rung 3) require explicit methodology and validation.

### Every Finding Needs "So What"
A finding without business impact mapping is an academic exercise, not consulting. Connect every insight to CX (customer experience), COST (operational efficiency), or EX (employee experience) outcomes.

### Human Judgment Is Required at Phase Transitions
AI augments analytical capacity — it does not replace decision-making authority. Phase transitions (DISCOVERY → ANALYSIS, ANALYSIS → SYNTHESIS, SYNTHESIS → DELIVERY) require human review and approval.

### Confidence Must Be Explicit
Never present uncertain findings as certain. Every finding carries a confidence level:
- **High (Validated):** Statistical significance confirmed, causal mechanism validated
- **Medium (Tested):** Statistical evidence present, causal mechanism plausible but not fully validated
- **Low (Hypothesis):** Pattern observed, insufficient evidence for action
- **Rejected:** Tested and found insufficient — document why

### The Best Analysis That Doesn't Reach the Decision-Maker Is Worthless
Executive summaries, clear visualizations, and audience-appropriate language are not optional. Analytical depth and communicative clarity are not in tension — they are complementary.

### Iteration Is Expected but Must Be Scoped
Backward flows in the project state machine are normal. But every iteration must be:
- **Scoped:** Clear boundaries on what's being revisited
- **Time-boxed:** Maximum effort before escalating
- **Logged:** Rationale recorded in decisions log

### Scaffolding Over Model
The value is in the architecture — the skills, standards, templates, and workflows — not in any single model call. Good scaffolding makes any capable model produce excellent results.

### Simpson's Paradox Is Real
Always check within-group vs. between-group correlations before claiming effects. Aggregate data regularly masks or reverses component-level relationships.
